# Taste rubric — 100 points, for comparative triage only

Each component receives an integer between zero and maximum. High scores are *prioritisation*, not proof of novelty, truth or importance. Score the exact question, not an inspiring parent theorem.

| Criterion | Max | High score requires | Low score signals |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Statement length | 12 | 1–3 crisp sentences with explicit quantifiers | Paragraph of caveats or notation |
| Prerequisites | 12 | Strong-school-student vocabulary and examples | Graduate technical machinery |
| Naturalness | 15 | Intrinsic operation/object, non-arbitrary threshold | Parameters chosen to manufacture effect |
| Sharpness | 10 | Binary yes/no or exact best value | Ill-defined "interesting behaviour" |
| Difficulty evidence | 20 | Several *documented* failed elementary attacks and bounded probes | Not attempted / trivial proof |
| Novelty audit grade | 15 | Deep, logged no-hit in original and alternate formulations, with limitations | Known exact/equivalent or not searched |
| Connections | 8 | Surprising links to canonical mathematical families | Isolated puzzle without motivation |
| Computational testability | 4 | Reproducible finite checks with an informative range | No useful test or unavailable model |
| Low triviality/degeneracy risk | 4 | Clean non-vacuous edge cases and no obvious shortcut | Collapse under small examples |
| **Total** | **100** | | |

**Mandatory vetoes override score:** known exact/equivalent; easy solution; false statement (unless reframed explicitly as a *new* well-defined question); inaccessible school-level wording; or dishonest evidence. A high rubric score is not an owner research selection. For candidates, log sub-scores and confidence/UNKNOWN separately.

## Calibration only — existing famous examples, NOT CND candidates

The following scores are **illustrative editorial judgements**, not literature audits or verified current difficulty. Novelty component is zero because these are named prior problems; not an assertion that all variants are known.

| Familiar example | Length /12 | Prereq /12 | Natural /15 | Sharp /10 | Difficulty /20 | Novelty /15 | Links /8 | Test /4 | Low-risk /4 | Total /100 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Collatz (3n+1 iterations) | 12 | 12 | 15 | 10 | 20 | 0 | 8 | 4 | 4 | 85 |
| Four colour (planar map colouring) | 12 | 12 | 15 | 10 | 0 | 0 | 8 | 4 | 4 | 65 |
| Erdős–Straus (4/n unit-fraction conjecture) | 10 | 11 | 14 | 10 | 18 | 0 | 8 | 4 | 3 | 78 |
| Happy ending (points in general position) | 9 | 9 | 14 | 9 | 18 | 0 | 7 | 4 | 3 | 73 |
| Hadwiger–Nelson (unit-distance plane colouring) | 8 | 10 | 14 | 10 | 20 | 0 | 8 | 3 | 4 | 77 |
| Earth–Moon (corresponding map colourings on two surfaces) | 9 | 10 | 13 | 9 | 15 | 0 | 7 | 4 | 3 | 70 |

**Calibrating remarks.** Collatz exemplifies a child-readable iteration and immense empirical temptation, but its statement is old. The four-colour theorem is solved, so resistance is *historical*, not present-tense open difficulty; the rubric penalises it accordingly. Erdős–Straus is an elegant exact Diophantine prescription (write 4/n as three positive unit fractions). The happy ending problem asks for forced convex configurations of planar points but its exact wording must specify general position and size. Hadwiger–Nelson asks how many colours the plane needs when points a unit apart must differ. Earth–Moon is a coupled-colouring archetype: corresponding countries/colonies constrain both maps, illustrating how a second interacting constraint can turn simple wording into surprising global structure. None of these calibrations authorises new candidate search, a mathematical status claim for a specific 2026 variant, or a verified new result.
