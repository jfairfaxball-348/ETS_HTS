# S014 — metric choice, retained edges and boundary growth

Attribution: John Fairfax-Ball's 2026-10-14 triangle point-insertion idea. These are elementary screening arguments and one provisional question, not a selected proof programme or an originality claim. CLM-0086..0091 record their scopes.

## Definitions and comparison (CLM-0086, CLM-0087)

Sites are distinct points in the Euclidean plane. Distance ties are resolved by lexicographic coordinates (x then y), independent of arrival order. A duplicate is rejected. A point has no direction: here the meaningful exceptional relation is collinearity with two specified endpoints, distinguished into segment interior and exterior ray. Corners mean strict turns, excluding a straight angle.

NG starts with a noncollinear triangle, keeps its three edges, and joins each new site to its nearest two previous sites, retaining all edges. Sites are vertices of the abstract graph; crossings do not automatically become sites. Edges are unordered pairs, not merged geometric traces. Thus V=3+t, E=3+2t, connected cycle rank E-V+1=t+1. If the drawing has no crossings, no vertex on a nonincident edge and no overlaps, Euler gives t+1 bounded faces. This does not count outer boundary corners. When there are c distinct transverse double crossings and no degeneracies, planarisation adds c vertices and 2c edges, so there are t+1+c bounded faces. Multiple concurrent crossings or overlaps need separate treatment.

SP keeps one boundary cycle: choose adjacent U,V, delete UV, replace it by UP,PV, and require a simple cycle, positive lengths and strict turns if claiming an extra corner. Adding edges without deleting UV is NG, not SP. Nonadjacent nearest sites do not define a single-edge replacement. For a strict interior insertion into a convex polygon, any boundary edge replacement is simple if P is noncollinear with its endpoints, but the nearest two vertices need not form an edge. For a nonconvex polygon, adjacency alone is insufficient; both segments must pass the nonintersection tests.

CH is the convex hull of arrived sites. An interior or segment-interior arrival has no effect on its strict vertices. An exterior arrival replaces a visible chain by two tangent segments, adding P and possibly deleting old vertices; the tangent endpoints need not be the nearest sites. A ray-collinear arrival can replace an extreme endpoint. CH forgets interior sites; SP can retain them as concave boundary vertices; NG retains interior sites and old chords. None of these translations is an equivalence. In particular, one cannot recover the arrival order or retained NG edge set from CH, or recover a chosen polygon cycle from NG.

If P lies on segment UV and UV is *subdivided*, V and E both rise by one; cycle rank and faces stay fixed and no strict corner is added. NG instead adds two abstract edges without deleting UV, which can overlap the existing trace. If P lies on the ray beyond V, inserting P between U,V in an SP cycle creates backtracking overlap UP with PV: this fails the simple-boundary rule. CH may replace V by P while removing other extremes, depending on the other sites. Collinearity does not automatically change any angle: interior subdivision leaves geometric corner angles unchanged.

## Mandatory calibration, done symbolically before CAM (CLM-0086)

Let A=(0,0), B=(10,0), C=(0,10).

* P=(12,2): squared distances to B,A,C are 8,148,208. PA intersects BC at A+(5/7)(P-A)=(60/7,10/7)=B+(1/7)(C-B), interior to both segments. P is outside ABC and on none of its supporting lines. Even deleting AB and attempting SP boundary A-P-B-C leaves PA crossing BC.
* P=(1,1): squared distances to A,B,C are 2,82,82. Lexicographic tie chooses C second. NG adds PA,PC, splitting the triangle into areas 5 and 45; the outer hull still has three corners. Replacing CA by CP,PA gives simple concave polygon A-B-C-P with four strict corners and area 45.
* P=(-1,-1): A=(5/6)P+(1/12)B+(1/12)C, so A is strictly inside PBC. Adding the exterior point replaces A and leaves a triangular hull; new point does not imply new hull corner count.
* P=(5,0): subdivision of AB has no new angle and no new bounded region. Keeping AB and adding AP,PB produces overlap in NG. For the extension P=(12,0), CH is A-P-C; B is no longer extreme and the hull again has three corners. Boundary A-P-B-C backtracks along PB and is invalid SP.

The above are calibration/known elementary facts and are NOT registered as new candidates. Graph/Euler/hull literature SRC-0068..0069 supports the standard context; SRC-0071 is an inspected short original proximity-graph paper, not the complete 2011 journal proof. Its definitions use neighbours in the whole static set, whereas NG uses predecessors and retains edges; no static crossing bound transfers automatically. SRC-0072 and SRC-0073 concern the one-predecessor random model, not the exact two-predecessor existential-order question.

## CND-0125 — nearest two are adjacent inside a convex polygon? (CLM-0088)

Universal claim FALSE. Take cyclic vertices A=(-10,0), B=(0,-1), C=(10,0), D=(0,1), and interior P=(1/10,1/10). Squared distances to D and B are 82/100 and 122/100; to C and A are 9802/100 and 10202/100. Thus B,D are the nearest two and are opposite, nonadjacent vertices. Convexity makes both segments visible, but does not supply the boundary adjacency needed for SP. KILLED_FALSE/DEGENERATE, not a new theorem.

Removing metric choice permits replacing any boundary edge at an interior point of a convex polygon. Removing the one-cycle boundary requirement permits adding both visible segments, with no new outer corner requirement. This is a genuine coupling but an elementary obstruction kills the universal statement.

## CND-0126 — can safe locations be described by finitely many straight lines? (CLM-0089)

YES by a finite Boolean combination of affine sign tests, so KILLED_EASY. Fix any simple polygon with n vertices and allow SP replacement only when the two nearest vertices are adjacent, the resulting cycle is simple and every boundary turn is nonzero. For candidate pair U,V, nearest-two membership compares squared distances with every other vertex W. The difference |P-U|²-|P-W|²=2P·(W-U)+|U|²-|W|² is affine in P. Resolve equality by the fixed lexicographic tie.

For each proposed segment PU or PV and old edge XY, the proper-crossing test uses products of orientation signs: orient(P,U,X), orient(P,U,Y), orient(X,Y,P), orient(X,Y,U). Those involving P are affine; the others are constant. Vertex-on-segment or overlap cases are handled by collinearity plus interval tests in x,y; these are also affine. Include positive-length / duplicate exclusions and nonzero-turn tests, all expressible by affine sign tests. Delete UV before testing and permit only the intended shared endpoints. Take the union of the successful adjacent-pair predicates. This describes EXACTLY the safe set in both directions, including tie boundaries and excluding degeneracies. It is a finite union of relatively open convex polygonal cells, including possible lower-dimensional cells and unbounded cells; not generally one convex region.

Use all vertex-pair supporting lines and perpendicular bisectors, with coordinate interval boundary lines as needed. There are O(n²) lines and O(n⁴) arrangement cells. This is an existence/conservative complexity description, NOT a sharp algorithmic bound. Order-2 Voronoi cells capture metric membership, but do not capture visibility or adjacency by themselves. SRC-0070 PDF was available at metadata level but its slides were NOT substantively parsed; the proof here is self-contained. No claimed source-native novelty.

## CND-0127 — does NG always have only linearly many crossings? (CLM-0090)

FALSE: worst-case number of proper edge-pair crossings is Θ(n²). The easy O(n²) upper bound follows from E=2n-3. Here is a finite constructive lower bound, using strict comparisons and a generic perturbation, not a numerical experiment.

Fix m>=1 and r=1/10. Consider first a *degenerate template* with seed A=(-2,0), B=(2,0), H1=(-r,0). Then insert Hj=((−r)^j,0), j=2,...,m+1. For j>=2 the nearest predecessor is Hj−1: its distance is (1+r)r^(j−1), while for any earlier Hk, k<=j−2, distance is at least (1-r²)r^(j−2), and both seed endpoints are farther. Thus the m horizontal segments Hj−1 Hj are retained. Second-neighbour choices are irrelevant to this subgraph.

Now insert Vk=(0,(−r)^(m+2+k)), k=1,...,m+1. For k>=2 the nearest predecessor is Vk−1, by the same strict scale comparison between vertical predecessors; every old horizontal site has distance at least r^(m+1), larger than any relevant vertical predecessor distance. Thus the m vertical segments Vk−1 Vk are retained. Every horizontal and every vertical segment contains the origin in its INTERIOR, so all m² horizontal-vertical pairs meet transversely.

For THIS fixed finite m, every specified nearest-predecessor inequality is strict and every specified proper crossing is transverse, hence all persist in some open neighbourhood of the template coordinates. Choose points in that neighbourhood generically, avoiding the finitely many polynomial equalities for collinear triples, distance ties and concurrent triple edge crossings. Such choices exist because these nonzero polynomial zero sets have empty interior; rational choices exist by density. The seed now becomes a noncollinear triangle, all sites have general position, and all m² selected crossings are proper and distinct. This makes a valid NG instance on n=2m+4 sites. Extra second-neighbour edges cannot remove retained crossings. Therefore linear total crossing bounds fail, and one edge can have arbitrarily many crossings. For odd sizes append one fresh generic site: retained crossings remain.

Scope: self-audited elementary screening proof. NOT claimed original; two-pass native priority coverage incomplete. The static fixed-k graph literature (SRC-0071) concerns a different object. This construction also shows why deleting time/order from the model changes the problem; the retained nearest-one subgraph alone already suffices for the quadratic obstruction. It does NOT disprove existence of a better order on the same unordered set.

## CND-0128 — can some arrival order always avoid crossings? (CLM-0091 for finite evidence only)

For every finite S of n>=3 distinct plane points with no three collinear, does there exist a permutation of S such that a triangle on its first three points, followed by NG nearest-two-predecessor insertion, is crossing-free? Distance ties use lexicographic coordinates. All sites stay fixed; all old edges stay; only seed and order may change. Plain version: **Can every collection of dots, with no three in a line, be added in some order so that joining each new dot to the nearest two earlier dots never crosses an old line?**

Status GENERATED only. Exact universal statement not resolved in S014. No claim of novelty, hardness or certified open status.

Elementary attack A: sort by x then y. FAILS on S={(0,0),(0,1),(1,2),(2,0)}: seed first three; squared distances from the last point to (0,0),(0,1),(1,2) are 4,5,5. The actual lex tie selects (0,1) second. The segment (2,0)-(0,1) crosses (0,0)-(1,2) at (2/5,4/5), interior to both. CAM independently marks lex order invalid and returns valid order [0,2,3,1]. Sorting does not settle the existential quantifier.

Elementary attack B: remove an extreme point, assume induction, then reinsert it. The prescribed calibration P=(12,2) is extreme in the full four-point hull but its nearest-two insertion into ABC crosses BC. Thus arbitrary extreme deletion plus arbitrary inductive order lacks the needed extension lemma. This is a failure of that specific proof strategy, not an obstruction to every seed/order. Edge count 2n-3 is below planar maximum 3n-6 but does not force a given drawing to be plane. A full minimum-weight/uncrossing or specially chosen deletion argument has NOT been completed; no theorem is inferred from these two failed attempts.
