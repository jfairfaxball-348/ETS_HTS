## The geometric last-vertex obstruction (CLM-0092)

**Lemma.** Let S be n>=3 distinct points in *strictly convex position* and let G be a crossing-free retained NG drawing generated by some arrival order. Then the *last* inserted site v must have as its two Euclidean-nearest other sites exactly its two immediate neighbours on the polygonal convex hull of S.

**Proof.** By its construction G has n vertices and E=3+2(n-3)=2n-3 distinct edges; an edge added to a new vertex cannot duplicate an old edge. Since every vertex is on the convex hull, a crossing-free straight-line graph on S is outerplanar, and has at most 2n-3 edges. If any convex-hull boundary segment ab were absent from G, that segment could be drawn without crossing existing edges: no edge runs outside the convex hull, every other site lies strictly to the interior side of line ab, and no three sites are collinear. Adding ab would create an outerplanar graph with 2n-2 edges, contradiction. Hence G contains every hull-side edge. For n>=4 the last vertex v was inserted after all others and receives *only* its two compulsory predecessor edges; later edges cannot attach because there are no later sites. Therefore its degree is exactly two. Its two hull-side edges must be those two compulsory edges, so its two closest predecessors—which are now all other sites—must be precisely its two hull neighbours. (For n=3 the triangle is trivially plane and each vertex's other two vertices are neighbours.) QED.

This condition is **necessary, not sufficient**; iterative removal demands more than it. It exploits the sharp **outerplanar** edge bound, not merely the weaker 3n-6 bound for general plane graphs. This is a self-contained elementary proof, not a claim of new theorem priority.

## Six-integer-site counterexample (CLM-0093)

Let the sites, in increasing x order, be:

| label | x | y | hull neighbours | two closest *other* sites |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| A | -40 | 400 | F, B | F, E |
| B | -22 | 121 | A, C | C, E |
| C | -20 | 100 | B, D | B, E |
| D | -4 | 4 | C, E | C, B |
| E | 24 | 144 | D, F | B, F |
| F | 28 | 196 | E, A | E, B |

All six points lie on y=x²/4. They have distinct x-coordinates and are exposed extreme points on the graph of a strictly convex function: at each x0, the tangent to the parabola through (x0,x0²/4) is a supporting line with all other selected points strictly above it. In particular the cyclic hull order is A,B,C,D,E,F, and no three sites are collinear. Independent consecutive hull-turn determinants are 180,144,4928,896,4352,15300 (all strictly positive). The hull closing edge is F-A.

The complete exact squared distance matrix, rows/columns A,B,C,D,E,F, is:

| d² | A | B | C | D | E | F |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 0 | 78165 | 90400 | 158112 | 69632 | 46240 |
| B | 78165 | 0 | 445 | 14013 | 2645 | 8125 |
| C | 90400 | 445 | 0 | 9472 | 3872 | 11520 |
| D | 158112 | 14013 | 9472 | 0 | 20384 | 37888 |
| E | 69632 | 2645 | 3872 | 20384 | 0 | 2720 |
| F | 46240 | 8125 | 11520 | 37888 | 2720 | 0 |

Every row has the unique indicated pair of minimum positive entries; lexicographic ties are immaterial here. In *each* row the nearest-two pair differs from that row's two immediate hull neighbours. Thus the lemma forbids every candidate for the last inserted site, so no valid crossing-free order exists. This establishes a concrete counterexample to the *universal* assertion for n=6, without any computational assumption. QED.
