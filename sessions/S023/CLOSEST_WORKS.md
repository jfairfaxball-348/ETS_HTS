# S023 — original theorem mappings, older bounds and ranked closest works

## Exact target
An optimally CHOSEN order on a finite strictly convex site set starts with three sites and at each arrival adds edges from the new vertex to its globally two closest earlier sites under Euclidean distance and prescribed lex tie selection; all edges remain, no proper crossings. CLM0099: such an order exists iff a COMPLETE recursive sequence deleting a metric hull ear exists. CLM0107: for EVERY n>=3 and EVERY n-point subset of EVERY ellipse of semiaxes a>=b>0 and ratio 1<=r=a/b<=R=sqrt(2/sin(2pi/5)-1), such an order exists. CLM0102: short directed elliptical chord endpoint extension for span <4 arctan(b/a), not arbitrary arcs. CLM0108: r=101/100 is explicitly universally good.

## Formal mappings (and their broken converses)
**Language A: chronological geometric graph.** In strict convex position with m vertices, there are exactly 3+2(m-3)=2m-3 retained edges. Planarity saturates the maximal outerplanar bound, requiring all hull edges. Hence last vertex in an order must attach to its two hull neighbours; compulsory closest-two ranking makes it a metric ear. Delete recursively to the initial triangle. Conversely any FULL metric-ear deletion certificate reversed yields a planar chronological graph; this precisely frames CLM0099. The classical ordinary ear condition is weaker: all convex polygon vertices have a polygon ear, but the S016 exact convex five-site no-metric-ear witness refutes ordinary-ear → our order. Thus classical ear-clipping is NOT a bidirectional equivalent.

**Language B: anisotropic circular metric.** Positive diagonal affine map A:(x,y)->(a x,b y) sends unit circle sites to the ellipse, and preserves segment crossing and cyclic hull order; squared Euclidean distance on ellipse pulls back to d_A(u,v)^2=a²(u_x-v_x)²+b²(u_y-v_y)². This gives a genuine BOTH-DIRECTIONS equivalence only if the circle's new metric d_A is retained. If one replaces it with ordinary round Euclidean distance, nearest rank and metric ears can change, so a round-circle theorem does NOT automatically prove ellipse CLM0107. The eccentricity transform e=sqrt(1-(b/a)^2)=sqrt(1-r^-2) is monotonically increasing on r>=1; the explicit uniform R threshold converts to e<=sqrt(1-R^-2), with e at ratio 101/100 exactly sqrt(201)/101. Searching only a/b numerical 1.050202 could miss equivalent e bounds. No inspected earlier numeric e threshold or uniform qualitative theorem found.

**Language C: static proximity or edge-greedy triangulation.** A static kNN graph ranks against all n points, while at each chronological arrival the target ranks ONLY earlier points. Edges to earlier neighbours can vanish in the final static kNN graph when later closer points arrive. Conversely, static kNN bounds do not produce an arrival-order certificate. Greedy polygon triangulation inserts shortest allowable compatible edge and may reject crossing edges; our incoming compulsory two edges may never be rejected. No established reduction in BOTH directions without additional constraints. Merely producing some triangulation of a convex polygon does not yield its arrival ranking.

**Quantifiers:** Each fixed n permits its own epsilon_n does not imply a single epsilon>0 valid simultaneously for all n (epsilon_n=1/n is the simple abstract countermodel to the invalid inference). CLM0107 has ∃epsilon>0 ∀n ∀n-site subsets, not ∀n ∃epsilon_n>0. The closed endpoint r=R is included; no minimum site separation required. A theorem using any finite n upper bound does not subsume it.
