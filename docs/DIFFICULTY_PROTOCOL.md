# Cheap difficulty kill battery

No question earns "hard" merely by surviving its author's first thought. Record *attempt, bounds, result and limitation* for each step, with CLM/SRC/CAM identifiers. Distinguish unattempted from failed, and strong from weak evidence.

1. **Finite brute-force / small case:** derive a canonical exhaustive search; preregister CAM before execution (model, ranges, code/hash, versions, stopping rule, raw outputs); check for missed degeneracies, hidden periodicity and tiny counterexamples. Never extrapolate proof past the bounds.
2. **Invariant or counting:** test parity, modular residues, degrees, double counting, extremal averages, pigeonhole, Euler-type arguments, monotone measures or potential functions. Record a *specific* attack and where it breaks.
3. **Direct theorem reduction:** check familiar school, olympiad and foundational research theorems; if established theorem gives an immediate answer, KILLED_EASY/KILLED_KNOWN as appropriate with exact conditions.
4. **Probabilistic / heuristic estimate:** estimate order of growth, random model, independence failure and contrary regimes; compare both pro and contra heuristics. HEURISTIC can motivate but not prove universality.
5. **Olympiad-day test:** attempt a strong-school-student proof (symmetry, induction, extremal choice, clever invariant), explicitly time-box the *test*, and record what was tried. A personal failure is weak evidence, never certification of research hardness.
6. **Literature check for an existing solution:** inspect standard references and verify exact hypotheses; same-looking theorem is not enough.
7. **Failure diagnosis:** score risks: finite-only issue, vacuous quantifier, all objects equal, trivial direct reduction, parameter transposition, cosmetically modified famous problem, or known computational sequence.

For SURVIVING, demand at least two nonidentical failed elementary attacks, nontrivial finite/heuristic evidence where applicable, no located immediate theorem, and an explicit uncertainty section. For purely infinite questions where brute force is not informative, record why and use constructive boundary cases. S001 executes none of this on candidates.
